How to Prepare for ICSE Class 10: A Practical Learn–Practise–Review Routine

A useful study plan is not a decorative timetable. It is a repeatable system that tells you what to learn, how to test it and what to fix next.

By Team MasterClassPublished: 11 November 2026Updated: 11 November 2026Reading time: 5 minutes

1. Start with the official syllabus

Use the current CISCE ICSE 2027 regulations and syllabus as your source of truth. Divide each subject into chapters and topics. Mark each topic as not started, learning, needs practice or ready for revision. Do not assume an older guidebook automatically matches the current syllabus.

2. Use a four-part study cycle

  1. Learn: study one defined concept from class notes, a textbook or a clear explanation.
  2. Recall: close the source and write or say the key ideas from memory.
  3. Practise: answer questions without looking at worked solutions first.
  4. Review: identify the reason for each mistake and schedule a short revisit.

3. Build a timetable around available time

Estimate the time you genuinely have after school, travel, meals, rest and other responsibilities. Give each session one outcome, such as completing a set of numericals or recalling a chapter's key terms. Leave flexibility for homework and unexpected tasks. A timetable that assumes every minute will be productive is not reliable.

4. Practise in the format you will be assessed

  • For numerical subjects, show working and check units, signs and final statements.
  • For descriptive subjects, practise structured answers using relevant terms and evidence.
  • For languages, practise writing, comprehension and prescribed-text questions regularly.
  • Use specimen papers and previous-year questions to understand formats, not to predict the exact paper.

5. Keep an error log

Record the topic, error type and next corrective action. Useful categories include concept gap, calculation slip, incomplete explanation, misread question and time-management issue. Reattempt a similar question later to check whether the correction worked.

6. Review progress weekly

Check which syllabus topics moved forward, which errors repeated and what needs attention next. If a plan repeatedly fails, reduce the number of tasks and make the next action smaller. Do not keep a timetable simply because it looks ambitious.

Practical rule: measure progress by topics understood and questions you can solve independently, not by hours spent with a book open.
